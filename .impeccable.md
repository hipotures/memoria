## Design Context

### Users
Primary users are operators and analysts exploring a personal screenshot corpus through semantic geography rather than raw search results. They need to understand the corpus from macro regions down to concrete screenshot evidence, while preserving orientation and context at every step.

### Brand Personality
Cartographic, analytical, deliberate.

The interface should feel like a semantic atlas and workbench rather than a flashy graph demo. It should evoke orientation, control, and interpretability. It should explicitly avoid force-directed spaghetti, dashboard clutter, and generic "AI map" aesthetics.

### Aesthetic Direction
Use a cluster-first atlas presentation with a stable spatial field, restrained bridges, and a persistent operational dock. The visual shell should feel editorial and technical at the same time: strong typographic hierarchy, clear regional grouping, and purposeful contrast between the immersive map surface and the read-heavy workbench panel. Region geometry should read as territory, not point-cloud noise.

### Design Principles
1. Geography first: the primary mental model is semantic distance and regional structure.
2. Workbench always available: selection should immediately produce actionable context in the dock.
3. Stable orientation: layout, labels, and camera behavior should preserve user memory across zooming and rebuilds.
4. Evidence over spectacle: motion and rendering should support interpretation, not decorative graph theatrics.
5. Progressive resolution: each drill-down level increases detail without changing the underlying model of interaction.
